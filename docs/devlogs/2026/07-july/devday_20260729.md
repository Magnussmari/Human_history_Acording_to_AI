### The era model

An era used to be a slice of the linear sweep and an event belonged to at most
one. That cannot express the corpus's own questions — the Haitian Revolution is
Age of Revolutions *and* Age of Abolition *and* modern, simultaneously. The
registry now carries four kinds in reserved id bands (chronological 1–50,
thematic 100–119, crisis 120–139, regional 140–159), each with `tone` driving
palette from data and `careLevel` marking the 7 eras covering atrocity,
enslavement or genocide. Overlap is legal by design; the validator enforces
everything else.

**Operator decision:** `careLevel: high` is a research-depth and
provenance requirement, **not** a publish gate.

### Research provenance

Angles and evidence are produced by different systems so nothing certifies
itself. Grok 4.5 writes falsifiable claims + a source-bias audit per era and is
barred from citations; a separate Scite MCP pass retrieves the literature and
returns verdicts.

It overruled the angle pass three times on the first run:

- African population decline 1700–1850: *supported* → **contested** (the
  offsetting term has never been estimated, so no net figure exists)
- Black Death mortality: 40–60% → the literature's **30–60%**; the urban>rural
  claim is close to inverted
- Classic Maya equinoctial alignments: *supported* → **contested** ("deeply
  rooted but unfounded")

98 DOIs across 3 dossiers; 6 spot-checked against Crossref, all real.

## The lesson: green CI proved nothing

Seven defects shipped or nearly shipped with the suite fully green. The suite
tested the routes it knew about, and `/era/[id]` was not one of them. Adding it
immediately caught two — including a **pre-existing** 320px overflow that had
been live and untested.

The worst was `findEraForYear`: its filter was `year <= e.start && year >= e.end`
against a registry that always stores `start < end`. Unsatisfiable — 0 matches at
every sampled year. Every `/year/[id]` page had been telling readers no scholarly
era covered that year, which the 2026-07-16 UX audit recorded as *missing
content*. It was a bug the whole time, and it made all 22 filed dossiers
unreachable from the timeline.

Second worst: **the first deploy deployed nothing.** Every route returned 200,
the script exited clean, and a container that had been running 11 days kept
serving the old build — because the runbook named `timeline-edge:v2` while
compose references `v12`. HTTP 200 cannot distinguish a fresh deploy from a stale
one. The script now derives the tag from compose and asserts the live era count
matches local.
